I · The Premise

The assets are intrinsic. The decision isn't.

In the AI economy, the scarce inputs are clean power, deep talent, trusted institutions and a place people want to live. Vancouver has all four. What it doesn't yet have is the decision to use them.

The AI build-out is the largest infrastructure race of the era, and it runs on things that cannot be conjured: abundant clean electricity, cool climates, research universities, global talent pipelines, and the quality of life that makes that talent stay. These are not policies. They are endowments — and Metro Vancouver holds a remarkable concentration of them.

Hydroelectric power among the cleanest on the continent. A research bench at UBC and SFU with decades of machine-learning pedigree. The deepest visual-effects, animation and gaming talent pool in the country. An immigration system that admits the engineers other jurisdictions turn away. And the Pacific position: the gateway between North American compute and Asian markets.

What the region lacks is not raw material but conversion: the anchor investments, the policy clarity, and the shared ambition to turn an enviable position into a defining industry. Toronto claimed the research crown. Seattle and the Bay Area claimed the capital. Vancouver's window to claim its own seat is open — and windows like this one do not stay open.

The Next Metro Vancouver: The AI Edition convenes the people who can make the conversion — the premiers and mayors, the utility and port executives, the founders, the chairs of the research bench, the institutional capital — for one editorially curated afternoon under the Urban Planet thesis: consequential decisions are made in cities, and this one is due.

II · The Agenda

Six questions, one afternoon.

The program is editorially curated — built like a magazine issue, not a trade show. Six questions that decide whether Vancouver becomes an AI city, each examined in a moderated fireside with the people closest to the answer.

1

The power question

AI runs on electricity, and BC's hydro is among the cleanest on Earth. Can the grid host the data centres and compute the industry needs — and should it? The utility, the regulators and the builders, at one table.

2

The talent question

UBC and SFU produce the researchers; the VFX, gaming and animation studios have built the deepest digital talent pool in Canada; immigration policy admits the engineers others turn away. How does the region keep that talent from boarding flights to Seattle and San Francisco?

3

The capital question

Research without anchor investment stays research. What it takes to land the flagship labs, the institutional capital and the first decisive bets — and what the region must offer to win them.

4

The application question

Vancouver's fastest path may not be building frontier models but applying AI to what the region already does at world scale: the port and its supply chains, the resource economy, film and media, life sciences. The companies doing it now, and what they need next.

5

The wildfire question

British Columbia's fire seasons now set the terms for entire summers. AI prediction models, satellite and sensor detection, and machine-directed response are moving from research to the front line — and no jurisdiction has more reason, or more terrain, to lead. Can BC become the proving ground that builds the wildfire technology the world will need?

6

The position question

Between Seattle's giants and Asia's markets, on the Cascadia corridor, in the time zone of the Pacific century: how Vancouver turns geography into strategy — partner to the giants without becoming their branch plant.

Curated by editors, not underwriters.

The Next Metro Vancouver is invitation-led. Seats are curated the way a correspondent builds a source list: for what each person knows, decides or controls — never for what they paid. The room is assembled from five constituencies:

Founders & Operators

Power & Infrastructure

Capital

Venture, pension and institutional investors weighing the region — including the ones who haven't committed yet.

The Research Bench

University leadership and the machine-learning researchers whose work the whole thesis rests on.

IV · The Partnership

What a partner actually gets.

Partnerships are limited by design — a small number per convening, each aligned with one of the six questions. Partners shape the questions on the table. They do not buy the answers.

Tier 01 · One per event

Title Partner

Your name on the convening itself. First position in all editorial and campaign material, a seat in the program's framing conversation, a private pre-event dinner with speakers, and a curated introduction list built from the room. The title partner is in the story, not beside it.

Tier 02 · One per question

Knowledge Partner

Ownership of one of the six questions: input on its framing, a voice in its fireside, and the post-event intelligence brief carrying your name to the full CityAge network.

Tier 03 · The premium instrument

Micro-Network

The most concentrated product CityAge makes: eight to twelve principals, personally curated by the publisher around your single objective, convened privately alongside the main event. No stage, no audience — just the right table.

Every tier includes presence in the event's editorial coverage and the post-event brief distributed to CityAge's network of 25,000 verified decision-makers. The partnership schedule, with investment levels, is available on request.
