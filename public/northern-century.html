The Manifesto

The future of the North unites the democratic nations of the northern hemisphere.

243,042 km Longest Coastline on Earth
66°N Arctic Circle

Canada. The United States. The Nordics. The Baltics. Northern Europe. One geography, one moment, one cause.

This is where the next century gets decided — telecommunications and data, AI, defense, climate, supply chains, and the critical minerals and metals the whole transition runs on. It all converges on the top of the world.

Canada is pivotal. The longest coastline on earth. A commanding position from the North Pole down to the rest of the world. For too long that was treated as a burden. It is the most consequential strategic asset in the hemisphere — and what Canada does with it sets the terms for everyone.

The window is open now. The sea lanes, the satellites, the defense spending, the race for minerals — all moving at once. The nations of the North decide how this is governed, or it gets decided for them.

What Gets Decided

Six forces shaping the North.

01 · Sovereignty

Arctic Claims & Strategic Control

Who controls the Northwest Passage, the Northern Sea Route, and the resources beneath them. The intersection of international law, military posture, and Indigenous rights in the world's last great territorial contest.

02 · Infrastructure

Ports, Corridors & Defence Build

Canada's $70B+ Arctic investment. New deep-water ports. Roads and rail into the High Arctic. The infrastructure programs that will define northern sovereignty for a century — and the capital structures that make them viable.

03 · Energy

Geothermal, Wind & Grid Independence

Iceland's volcanic grid. Scandinavian offshore wind. The energy systems that allow remote Arctic settlements and military installations to operate independently of southern supply chains — and the investment thesis behind them.

04 · Trade

Nordic-Atlantic Bilateral Networks

The Canada-Europe trade corridor. Nordic-North American industrial partnerships. How changing ice conditions are redrawing the economics of global shipping — and which ports, corridors, and nations stand to benefit most.

05 · Space

Earth Observation & the Space Economy

Arctic monitoring from orbit. The role of Earth observation in sovereignty claims, climate tracking, and resource mapping. How the space economy — satellites, launch, data — is inseparable from the future of northern infrastructure.

06 · Capital

Sovereign Wealth & Institutional Investment

The pension funds and sovereign wealth vehicles moving capital into Arctic infrastructure. How institutional investors are pricing sovereignty risk. The financial architecture funding the Northern Century — and who is positioned now.

Why Now
"The Arctic is not the edge of the map. It is the next centre of gravity for capital, sovereignty, and the infrastructure that determines how nations project power."
— Miro Cernetig, CEO & Publisher, CityAge
Climate is opening new corridors. The Northwest Passage is navigable for longer each year. New shipping routes are being assessed — and new military infrastructure built to control them.
Canada has committed $70B+ to Arctic sovereignty. That capital is looking for industrial partners, advisors, and intelligence on where the opportunity is real.
NATO's northern flank is the new strategic frontier. Sweden and Finland's accession has changed the alliance's geography. Defence infrastructure is being built at pace.
Critical minerals are the new oil. The Arctic holds significant reserves of rare earths, lithium, and nickel powering the energy transition — and competition for them is intensifying.
